**Highlight 5 of 6** from the Sept 17 Commission worksheets.

## What commissioners were asked

A second worksheet asked members to consider whether various <Term id="medicaid">Medicaid</Term> benefits primarily:

- keep people **healthy and safe**,
- support a **full life**,
- do **both**, or
- need **more information**.

<Term id="personal-care">Personal care</Term> and <Term id="homemaker">homemaker</Term> under <Term id="cfc">CFC</Term> — including <Term id="paid-family-caregiving">paid family caregiving</Term> — were specifically included in that exercise.

## Critical framing (do not miss this)

The worksheet describes this as a **first reading** — **not** a decision and **not** a vote to cut any service.

## Plain English

Policymakers are sorting benefits into “must have for safety” vs. “helps someone live a fuller life.” That sorting can precede later budget fights. Including CFC personal care / homemaker / paid family care means those services are in the conversation — but the Sept 17 materials were not themselves a cut vote.

## Why a newcomer should care

This is the political risk channel for <Term id="pprs">PPRS</Term>-type services. Provider advocacy focuses on cost and quality solutions so “sort and cut” is not the only path.
