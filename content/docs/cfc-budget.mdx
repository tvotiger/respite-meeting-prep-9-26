## What happened

<Term id="cfc">CFC</Term> had been projected to **save $45 million** in <Term id="general-fund">General Fund</Term> spending — largely because of a richer <Term id="federal-match">federal match</Term>.

In practice, <Term id="homemaker">homemaker</Term> and <Term id="personal-care">personal-care</Term> <Term id="utilization">utilization</Term> grew beyond what that enhanced federal match could offset. <Term id="hcpf">HCPF</Term> identified that utilization growth as the **primary contributor** to the budget problem.

## Who was using more care

CFC also widened two delivery paths:

| Path | What it means here |
| --- | --- |
| <Term id="paid-family-caregiving">Paid family caregiving</Term> | Family members (e.g. parents) getting paid for authorized supports |
| <Term id="agency-delivered-care">Agency-delivered care</Term> | Agencies sending workers (the <Term id="pprs">PPRS</Term>-style model) |

The worksheet associated growth differently by service:

- **Homemaker growth** — adults who already had <Term id="waiver">waiver</Term> access but had not been using homemaker before.
- **Personal-care growth** — children whose parents may now be paid.

## Why a newcomer should care

This is the core “why is <Term id="hcbs">HCBS</Term> in the news?” story: a program sold partly as a General Fund saver became a General Fund spender when real-world use outran the forecast.

A provider-side response is not “deny the cost.” It is “fix oversight, admin complexity, and proportional rules before defaulting to cuts.” See [Why this matters](/docs/why-it-matters).
